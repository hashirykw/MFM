f. 02 — How an answer earns its marks

Knowing the story is the first rung, not the answer.

One Paper 1 question, four answers, each a rung higher. Scroll and watch the facts light up as the answer climbs from a general story to a detailed, referenced account, and then to a lesson for today.

The Battle of Badr

Fact file: fought on 17 Ramadan, 2 AH (624 CE) near the wells of Badr. About 313 Muslims faced about 1,000 Makkans, led by Abu Jahl, who was killed. Allah promised help with a thousand angels (Sura 8.9). Prisoners could earn their freedom by teaching ten Muslims to read. The lesson: faith, unity and obedience matter more than numbers.

(a) Write an account of the Battle of Badr. [10]
(b) What can Muslims today learn from it? [4]

AO1Recall
AO1Detail
AO1Evidence
AO2Significance
Low level01 · Recall

The Battle of Badr was the first major battle between the Muslims and the Quraysh of Makka. The Muslims were few but they won.

True, but general. A short story with no detail stays in the low levels.
Mid level02 · Detail

It was fought on 17 Ramadan, 2 AH. The Makkans marched to protect Abu Sufyan’s caravan, and about 313 Muslims met an army of about 1,000 at the wells of Badr, where Abu Jahl was killed.

Dates, numbers and names move a part (a) answer up the levels.
Top level03 · Evidence

The Prophet prayed through the night, and Allah answered that He would help with a thousand angels (Sura 8.9). After the victory, prisoners who could read were freed for teaching ten Muslims, showing his mercy and his value for knowledge.

A detailed, well-ordered account backed by the Qur’an reaches the top level for 10 marks.
Part (b)04 · Significance

Badr shows that faith and obedience count for more than numbers. A Muslim today who faces a struggle, such as standing up for what is right when friends disagree, prepares fully and then trusts Allah, as the Prophet did.

Part (b) wants a lesson for Muslims now, with an example from life today, not more history.

f. 04 — The method

Learn it, order it, prove it, apply it.

How every topic runs in Sir Fazil’s class, whether you sit in Karachi, join online or follow the recorded course.

  1. 01Passages

    Learn the 15 passages by theme

    Each passage is learned under its theme, so in Question 1 you write the themes in your own words and link them to Muslim life today.

    • God in Himself
    • God’s relationship with the created world
    • God’s Messengers
  2. 02Seerah

    The Prophet’s life, in order

    Makka to Madina, from 570 to 632, with the dates, the numbers and the names that examiners look for.

    • Birth to the first revelation
    • Persecution, Hijra and Madina
    • Badr, Uhud, the Trench, Hudaybiyya
  3. 03Evidence

    Back every point with the Qur’an or a Hadith

    A reference turns a good answer into a top-level answer. Students learn which verse or Hadith supports which point.

    • Linked verses for every theme
    • The 20 set Hadiths
    • Quoting accurately and briefly
  4. 04Part (a)

    Write the 10-mark account

    Accurate, detailed and in order. Part (a) rewards what you know, so the account has to be complete, not long.

    • Plan in under a minute
    • Names, dates, numbers
    • No padding
  5. 05Part (b)

    Explain why it matters today

    Four marks for understanding: what the event or teaching means for a Muslim now, with a real example from life today.

    • Significance, not history
    • One developed example
    • Model answers to compare
  6. 06Exam

    Past papers by topic, then in full

    Practised against the mark scheme and timed to the real length, so 90 minutes feels normal on the day.

    • Mark schemes and examiner reports
    • Exam timer with focus mode
    • Topical past-paper books

f. 07 — The examiner’s vocabulary

Answer the question that was asked.

The command word tells you whether the marks are for knowledge, in part (a), or for understanding, in part (b). Sir Fazil drills both until the switch is automatic.

Outline

Set out the main events or points briefly, in order. Breadth first.

Part (a) · knowledge
Describe

Say what happened or what the teaching is, with detail: who, when, where, how many.

Part (a) · knowledge
Write an account

A full, ordered narrative with names, dates and references from the Qur’an or Hadith.

Part (a) · 10 marks
Explain

Give the reason: why it happened, or why Muslims believe or act this way.

Part (a) or (b)
Why is it important

Its significance for Muslims and for the faith, not a repeat of the facts.

Part (b) · understanding
What can Muslims learn

A lesson for today, developed with a real example from life now.

Part (b) · the top band
