The method

Three things the mark scheme rewards.

Every class, whether in Karachi, online or recorded, is built around how Cambridge awards marks in Paper 1 and Paper 2.

i.

Know it in order

The 15 passages by theme, the Seerah from Makka to Madina, the Caliphs and the Pillars, with the dates, numbers and names examiners look for.

ii.

Back it with evidence

The right verse or Hadith for each point, quoted briefly and accurately. A reference turns a good answer into a top-level one.

iii.

Explain why it matters

Part (b) is four marks for understanding: what an event or teaching means for a Muslim today, with a real example, not more history.

A worked example

One question, four answers.

Each answer climbs one level. Open them in turn and watch which facts the examiner is looking for.

Paper 1 style · Question 3

The Battle of Badr

Fought on 17 Ramadan, 2 AH near the wells of Badr. About 313 Muslims faced about 1,000 Makkans. Allah promised help with a thousand angels (8:9). Prisoners earned their freedom by teaching ten Muslims to read. The lesson: faith and obedience count for more than numbers.

(a) Write an account of the Battle of Badr.[10]
(b) What can Muslims today learn from it?[4]
